The story

It worked perfectly

In the Long Afternoon of the world, when the sun had grown old and red and was in no particular hurry, there stood a crooked tower called the Quire. It belonged to Ozimand Vey the Elaborate, a magician of considerable reputation, most of which he had written himself.

His apprentice, Fennick Tumbril (junior apprentice, under-scullion and part-time hat stand), had a problem. The Midsummer Revel was in three days, and Fennick had a face like a damp Tuesday. So, while his master was away, Fennick borrowed the grimoire. Just to look.

The Philtre of Irresistible Hex Appeal called for a lark's tongue. Fennick, whose handwriting was as bad as his reading, used a frog's.

It worked perfectly. On the frog.

That same night, the Conclave of the Unremarked came calling. Fennick was swept down the scullery drain, and woke at the very bottom of the Undercroft: halls older than the tower, older than anyone's memory, and currently full of other people's monsters.

To be human again, he must climb. Up through the dark. Up to the Workshop. And brew the cure before his master gets home.

What's in it

One frog, eight levels, a lot of stairs

All eight levels are in the test build, and the tuning goes on. Our own game, inspired by the 8-bit classics we grew up on.

Rooms in the dark

Every room is dark until you hop in. Then the torches catch, and you see what's waiting for you. Usually something bored, and occasionally something with cutlery.

Spells from runes

Win runes, old letters cut in glowing slate, and spend them on spells: stronger bolts, a shield, more power, and tricks for finding hidden doors.

Rival magicians

Each level hides one of the Conclave of the Unremarked: grand, absurd magicians who are furious that nobody writes about them. Catch one for a duel of wits, and take their runes.

Plan your route

Creaky old lifts, glowing floor glyphs and drains with a long way down join the levels up. Climb steadily, take a risky shortcut, or slide back down with a splosh.

A firefly for a friend

A second player can join as Glim, Fennick's firefly: lighting rooms early and zapping small things. Glim can't be hurt, only get dizzy, for a friend who'd rather light the way than lead it.

Painted, 16-bit style

Stone, moss and torchlight painted pixel by pixel in the style of the 16-bit classics, using the Next's full palette, over a smoothly scrolling dungeon.

Music on three chips

An adventurous score on all three of the Next's sound chips, with drums, that swells into battle when the monsters notice you and calms down when they've stopped.

It talks

A narrator who can't resist a joke, and rival magicians who taunt you out loud. "Ah. A frog. How... unprecedented."

Dark fantasy, dry wit

A properly creepy Undercroft, rivals who are pompous and genuinely dangerous, and a narrator who has seen it all. For anyone who remembers the 8-bit days, and anyone who wishes they did.

Download

Try the test build

It's free. A folder for a ZX Spectrum Next (any model) or an emulator: the game and the files it reads beside it. All eight levels, the duels, the ending and the rounds after it, but it's still being tuned, so expect changes. Found a bug or have an idea? Reply on the Facebook post where you found it, and say where you were and what happened.

Latest build: v152, 10 November 2026, 1098 KB. What's new in it

Download the zipOn itch.io
  1. On a real Next: unzip it and copy the whole hexappeal folder to your SD card. Open it in the Browser, pick hexappeal.nex and press ENTER. Keep the other files beside it: they're the levels, the paintings and the speech.
  2. In CSpect: give it the folder as its SD card (-mmc= the folder) and open hexappeal.nex. Start it with -esc so Escape doesn't close the emulator.
  3. Playing: Q A O P to hop (or the cursor keys, or a Kempston joystick), SPACE to fire. Hold fire on a glyph to use it, or anywhere else to cast your effect spell. In a duel, left and right pick a letter and fire swaps it. H pauses. K on the title sets your own keys.
  4. Two players: a friend presses 2 to join as Glim, with the cursor keys and 0 to fire, or a second Kempston joystick.

Changelog

What's new

10 November 2026, later
  • Much more animation: the Obliging Imp really flies, the magicians breathe and work up their witchlight, explosions burst into embers and smoke, and Fennick glances, gulps and blinks while he waits. The creatures, bolts and the magicians' walks and casts have twice the frames, so they move more smoothly.
  • A weary frog: when his energy runs low, Fennick slumps, sags and sighs.
  • Crowds keep their distance: creatures never pile on top of each other, and a crowded room costs the Next much less work.
  • Levels with two chests (the Crown Cellars and the Workshop) let you open both in one visit.
10 November 2026
  • The first test build is out: download it above.
  • Rounds: beat the ending and the climb starts again from the Root Halls, harder, in new colours, up to round 8, with bonuses for clearing the Conclave.
  • Your rivals' faces: each magician of the Conclave has a painted portrait in the duel, and the minor ones a hooded apprentice.
  • A sound for everything: a croak as Fennick hops, a heartbeat when he's low, a grunt from an enraged creature, booms for room blasts and dying generators, and its own sound for every spell, lift, chest and swap in the duel. And a "Wheee! Splosh." between levels.
  • Levels remember what you did: go up a level and come back, and the generators you destroyed are still rubble, chests stay open, spent Power glyphs stay spent and crystals stay taken. Your saved game keeps it all too.
  • Pause with H, and quit to the title from there. Your own keys can't take H or 2 any more.
  • Game over shows your whole score, long rivals' names fit the duel screen, and the title tells you if the files beside the game are missing.
  • Fixes: a few creatures (the Mope by the Delvers' Gate among them) could never be hit by a bolt; the title no longer flashes in odd colours while the prologue loads; Quill is properly gold.
31 October 2026
  • The Root Halls and the Drowned Library: the first two levels are real at last, with Pelligrew the Lesser and Dame Ossory waiting at the end of each.
  • Fennick leaps: he springs up in an arc over his own shadow, instead of hopping in place.
  • The Obliging Imp: the missing effect spell, a little helper that fights beside you for 20 seconds.
  • The Twin Magisters now duel you together, in two rounds, finishing each other's sentences.
  • Generators can be destroyed with your bolts, with a spark on every hit. Before, the bolts just stopped at their edge.
  • New art: a cage lift and drain grate, opened chests, spent Power glyphs, a bolt that grows with your Offence, shabby minor magicians, and a drooping frog when energy runs low.
  • A tidier panel: the bars line up with the text, and EFF lights green when your effect spell is ready.
  • Fennick can no longer get boxed in by a generator's hazards, and the prologue says "under-scullion" again.
30 October 2026
  • A proper spell book: leaf through every spell, read what each one does, see how hard it will drain you, and cast back down when your power can't keep up. The panel is tidier too, with the drain on show.
  • Fennick really hops now: a full squash-and-stretch jump in every direction, and he blinks and gulps while he waits. Every creature has its own walk, sigh, stamp or sneeze.
  • Eight levels on the climb up the Quire, each with its own rival magician of the Conclave.
  • The painted title, a spoken prologue and ending, and a new cut-away Travel screen.
  • The duel of wits no longer locks up, and the level music keeps one steady pace.
29 October 2026
  • The base game hops: Fennick's first level of the Undercroft, room by dark room, with ghosts to zap and glow-crystals to top up his power.
  • The painted art and the music are made, and moving in next.
